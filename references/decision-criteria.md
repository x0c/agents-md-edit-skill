# Decision criteria

The unit of preservation is an effective future behavior with its scope and exceptions, not inherited sentences or layout.

## Value and placement

For each instruction establish:

- **Decision:** Which future action changes? Remove prose that changes none, or retain useful background in its owning record. A rule and its inverse are one rule: once a priority is stated, warnings against the displaced alternatives change no action. Past costs justify a rule to its author; they change no future action — but a consequence naming irreversible damage or third-party harm is a constraint and stays, and a user-decision marker with its date is part of the adopted constraint, never a cost.
- **Reader:** The reader is a competent model. What it can derive on its own — standard tool mechanics, generic symptom patterns, common cause-effect chains, recovery after a confirmed finding — adds no information. Keep the named local fact (which tool, which mode, which state); drop the causal explanation and symptom fingerprints derivable from it.
- **Authority:** Did current user intent decide this rule? Evidence and incidents explain rules; they never authorize them.
- **Size:** The rule enters the file at the size of the decided content: restate the decided action with its trigger plus at most one pointer; the source narrative's size is not the rule's size, and explicitly requested size or detail is decided content. State the check, not the fix: what follows a confirmed finding is derivable. Commands, paths, procedures and quirks follow the Owner rule to their owning doc; with no owning doc, omit them rather than inline. Verified detail earns no default-loaded space.
- **Scope:** Preserve applicable tasks, exceptions, numbers, paths and identifiers when they change the action. Do not generalize examples or narrow a category to one named instance. A category with a testable boundary (range, suffix, protocol class) is the scope; an appended catalog derivable from that boundary is enumeration, not scope; a list with no derivable pattern, or one serving as the routing key, is itself scope.
- **Owner:** Global rules govern cross-product behavior; project rules govern a product; skills own reusable procedures; shared guides own platform knowledge; generated contracts change through their source. An edited file's embedded capture policy names the owning records (never the edited rule file itself) for symptoms, causes and evidence; it never enlarges the rule body itself.
- **Recoverability:** Test whether a fresh agent can find a current authority cheaply. Derivable facts can move; hidden constraints, rationale a model cannot infer, and instructions to consult that authority may still be necessary.
- **Loading:** Keep default context when it changes decisions across most tasks, or when omission risks a consequential mistake before retrieval. Move narrow detail behind an accurate content summary only after checking that its destination actually owns it. A destination pointing back to AGENTS.md cannot replace the rule.

Global placement asks who needs it, who can decide it and what omission breaks. Product names, dated handoffs, wave ledgers and product-specific requirements usually belong in product docs. Cross-product but infrequent knowledge belongs in indexed shared guides. Move the body rather than copy it; keep a pointer when it serves retrieval.

## Structure and compression

Map rules independently of old headings. Give each a disposition and concrete issue: repetition, misplacement, stale history, excessive enumeration or conflicting absolutes. Merge repeated requirements into one authority. Keep precision that changes behavior; remove redundant synonym lists, incident narrative, warnings that restate a stated priority, and repeated emphasis. Historical dates and examples belong in the relevant record unless their identity still changes the decision — a user-decision marker's date always does. In drafted rules, a parenthetical aside that hedges or qualifies marks undecided content — fold it into the decision or drop it; one carrying a decided exception or the allowed pointer stays, folded into the sentence.

Group larger files by the decisions governed. Avoid miscellaneous umbrellas that hide unrelated rules, but do not force one heading per sentence in a small file. A preservation ledger is useful for coverage and cannot veto better organization. A full review covers all sections and interactions; a bounded change expands only for relevant overlaps or contradictions.

## Navigation

Index entries describe contents and where authority lives. One shared relevance instruction establishes reading before governed decisions. Do not encode quotas, exhaustive trigger lists or prohibitions inside descriptions. Requirements should be found from an ordinary implementation request, not only after a symptom appears.

Authority has one body; references form a graph. Multiple useful routes to the same document are valid. Check zero-inbound orphans rather than treating every repeated reference as duplication. Direct links are the default; group only when navigation is hard to scan. Counts and line lengths prompt judgment rather than force restructuring.

## Verification

Compare the changed action, scope, timing and exceptions across ordinary, exception and unrelated tasks. Apply the deletion test to new sentences: remove it; if no future action changes, it stays removed. Check actual link targets, generated markers and surrounding rules. If user-confirmed requirements supersede old evidence, reconcile the affected authority; mark unresolved conflicts instead of silently choosing.

When a rule still fails, distinguish wording, navigation, execution and tool coverage. Correct the demonstrated cause instead of adding a universal prohibition per incident. Structural checks and compact stamps measure limited coverage; they do not prove semantic preservation or improved agent performance.
